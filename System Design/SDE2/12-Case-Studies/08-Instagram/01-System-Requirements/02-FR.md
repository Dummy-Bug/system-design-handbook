# Instagram Functional Requirements

## The core flows

Instagram at its simplest is four things working together.

The first flow is **upload** — a user takes a photo or short video and posts it. The media gets stored, a post record is created, and it becomes visible to that user's followers. The upload path is a write-heavy operation: media goes to object storage, metadata goes to the database, and the post needs to fan out to followers' feeds.

The second flow is **the home feed** — the main screen. A user opens the app and sees a ranked list of posts from accounts they follow, newest first. This is the core design challenge of Instagram: at scale, generating this feed for hundreds of millions of users is expensive. The naive approach breaks quickly.

The third flow is **stories** — ephemeral content that disappears after 24 hours. A user posts a story; their followers can see it for 24 hours, then it's gone. The key difference from a regular post is the TTL — storage needs to clean it up automatically, and the feed for stories is separate from the main post feed.

The fourth flow is **profile** — viewing any user's page. You see their post grid, their follower count, and their following count. This is a read-heavy path — public profiles get hit constantly.

Alongside these are **engagement** (liking and commenting on posts) and **follow/unfollow**, which are the actions that make the feed meaningful.

---

## What's in scope

- **Upload** — post a photo or short video; it appears on your profile and fans out to followers' feeds
- **Home feed** — paginated list of posts from followed accounts, ordered by recency
- **Stories** — 24-hour ephemeral posts, visible to followers, auto-deleted after TTL
- **Profile** — user's post grid, follower count, following count
- **Explore feed** — personalised content from accounts the user doesn't follow (served from pre-computed recommendations, not designed from scratch here)
- **Engagement** — like and comment on posts
- **Follow / unfollow** — the social graph action that controls whose posts appear in your feed

## What's out of scope (and why you deferred it)

### Direct Messages

DMs are a real-time messaging problem — WebSockets, delivery guarantees, inbox management. That's an entirely different architecture from feed generation. The WhatsApp case study covers this in full. Keeping it out of Instagram lets the feed design stay focused.

### Private accounts and follow-request approval

Private accounts add an approval layer to the follow graph. Before fan-out runs, you'd need to check whether a follow is pending or approved. At 500M DAU, that extra check touches every single feed write. It's not hard, but it's a detail that clutters the core design. Deferring it keeps the fan-out architecture clean.

### Reels transcoding pipeline

Full Reels means raw video uploaded by the user gets transcoded into multiple resolutions and served over HLS/DASH with adaptive bitrate streaming. That's the Netflix architecture — a separate ingestion, transcoding, and CDN delivery pipeline. For Instagram we treat video as a media blob stored in object storage, same as a photo. The storage and delivery design stays the same; the transcoding complexity is out of scope.

### Notifications

When someone likes your photo or follows you, a notification fires. That's a fan-out through a notification service — Kafka topics, APNs/FCM delivery, retry logic. The Notification System case study covers this in full. Here we note it exists and cross-reference it; we don't redesign it.

### Search (hashtags, usernames)

Search is an inverted index problem — a completely different read path. Adding it here would split the design between feed architecture and search infrastructure. Out of scope; the Search Engine case study handles it.

---

> [!tip] Interview framing
> The four flows — upload, home feed, stories, profile — are the obvious answer. What makes a strong answer is knowing *what shapes the architecture*. The home feed is the hard part: fan-out on write vs fan-out on read is the central design decision for Instagram. Everything else — stories TTL, explore feed pre-computation, engagement writes — is straightforward once you've resolved the feed problem. Out-of-scope items are worth naming briefly: DMs (async messaging, separate system), Reels transcoding (Netflix-style pipeline), notifications (cross-reference the Notification System), search (inverted index, separate system). You don't need to design them — you need to know *why* they're separate.
